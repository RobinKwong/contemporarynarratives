## MapReader resources and further reading {#mapreader}

Explore the tools, datasets and research behind **MapReader**, an open-source computer vision framework developed for analysing historical maps and other image collections at scale.

### Learn about the research project behind MapReader

- **[Living with Machines](https://livingwithmachines.ac.uk/)**  
  A pioneering interdisciplinary research project exploring how technology transformed everyday life during the Industrial Revolution. MapReader was originally developed as part of this initiative to help researchers analyse thousands of historical maps using AI and machine learning techniques.

### Explore the historical map collections

- **[National Library of Scotland Historic Maps](https://maps.nls.uk/os/)**  
  Access extensive digitised collections of Ordnance Survey maps of England, Wales and Scotland. These historical maps form one of the key datasets used in MapReader research.
- **[Georeferenced Historical Map Viewer](https://maps.nls.uk/geo/explore/)**  
  Compare historic maps with contemporary geography through an interactive map explorer. A useful resource for understanding the types of materials that MapReader can analyse.

### Access the software

- **[MapReader GitHub repository](https://github.com/maps-as-data/MapReader)**  
  The main open-source software repository containing the MapReader codebase. The platform supports image classification, feature detection and text extraction workflows for historical maps and other image datasets.

### Documentation and getting started

- **[MapReader documentation](https://mapreader.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)**  
  Comprehensive guides covering installation, tutorials, workflows, API references and best practices for using MapReader in research and creative applications.

### Examples and tutorials

- **[MapReader examples repository](https://github.com/maps-as-data/mapreader-examples)**  
  A collection of worked examples, Jupyter notebooks and reproducible workflows demonstrating how to use MapReader for both geospatial and non-geospatial image analysis projects.

### Research publications

- **[Patch-based classification for historical maps](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.06857.pdf)**  
  Learn about the machine-learning concepts that influenced MapReader's approach of classifying image patches rather than relying exclusively on pixel-level segmentation.
- **[Combining text recognition and map analysis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/epdf/10.1080/03085694.2024.2453336?needAccess=true)**  
  Research demonstrating how MapReader combines OCR and image classification techniques to discover patterns within historical map collections.
- **[Beyond historical maps](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40475908/)**  
  An example of how MapReader-inspired methods can be applied in other domains, including plant phenotyping and scientific image analysis.

## Computational humanities and AI resource guide {#computational-humanities}

Explore a selection of projects, datasets and research tools demonstrating how computational methods can be used to study culture, history, literature and censorship. You will find materials that:

- Demonstrate how computational methods can uncover patterns hidden in large cultural collections.
- Show how AI can be used both as a research tool and as an object of critical investigation.
- Connect digital humanities, archival research, data science and creative technologies.
- Inspire new approaches to working with large-scale textual and cultural datasets.

### Historical newspapers and spatial analysis

- **[Mapping Victorian Accidents 1 (animated visualisation)](https://docs.google.com/file/d/13NQmRn-WV0tzcnB8YoLOAtQedAbOCWzV/preview)**  
  A visualisation of accidents extracted from nineteenth-century newspapers, built using place-name detection and disambiguation to locate accident reports geographically. It shows how accidents can be analysed across both time and space using computational methods applied to historical newspaper collections.
- **[Mapping Victorian Accidents 2 (second case-study visualisation)](https://docs.google.com/file/d/1Nt9fRCqRytK0d0yJgLZZnWVmeagm3_fs/preview)**  
  A companion visualisation from the same Victorian accidents project. It demonstrates large-scale analysis of historical newspaper archives and illustrates how computational approaches can reveal patterns and narratives hidden within vast textual collections.

### Censorship, queer archives and digital collections: AI, language models and cultural data

Investigate how large language models interpret, classify and moderate literary texts. Explore practical examples of prompt design, content classification and bias analysis, and reflect on the role of AI systems as cultural gatekeepers and their influence on access to information.

- **[Seized Books Gallery](https://exhibitions.london.ac.uk/s/seizedbooks/page/gallery)**  
  An online gallery from the University of London's **Seized Books** project. It showcases books confiscated during "Operation Tiger", a UK customs campaign targeting imported LGBTQ+ literature, and provides access to digitised materials from the Cutbill Collection, exploring questions of censorship, sexuality, cultural value and archival preservation.
- **[Seized Books Research Notebook (Google Colab)](https://colab.research.google.com/drive/194m5zWx3nx7jbVtK_tu7yxAijrhr06S8?usp=sharing)**  
  A computational notebook associated with the Seized Books project, used to investigate how large language models respond to and classify passages from LGBTQ+ literature. It supports research on AI moderation, censorship, bias, moral reasoning and content classification.
